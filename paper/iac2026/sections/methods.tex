\section{ARTPS Methodology}
This section describes the ARTPS pipeline as implemented for the present study.
Quantitative performance claims appear only in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{System overview}
ARTPS ingests a single RGB field image and produces a ranked list of candidate targets, or an
explicit no-selection outcome when evidence is insufficient.
Primary modules are: (i)~multi-cue anomaly responses, (ii)~monocular relative depth,
(iii)~multi-cue fusion (entropy-weighted specification as the adaptive fusion mode),
(iv)~candidate localization, (v)~operational false-positive
suppression, (vi)~apparent size / image-relative size--distance policy,
(vii)~feature-space diversity, (viii)~Priority Buffer, and (ix)~runtime fail-safe behaviour
with evaluation-integrity checks.
The image-level ARTPS score is defined as the maximum score among candidates that remain
valid after operational filtering.
The human-reviewed validation uses this aggregation.
Curiosity ranking, feature-space diversity, and the Priority Buffer are operational
selection mechanisms; they are \emph{not} applied on the image-level score.
Optional Mars-oriented enhancement may precede cue extraction; it is preprocessing, not a
detection metric.
Optional super-resolution enhancement was disabled in all reported experiments.

\subsection{Multi-cue visual anomaly detection}
ARTPS forms visual anomaly responses from embedding- or reconstruction-oriented cues aligned
with industrial anomaly practice~\citep{defard2020padim,roth2022patchcore}.
A reconstruction residual emphasizes appearance that the autoencoder cannot explain from
typical terrain context.
Complementary texture and local-contrast cues capture high-frequency structure that a
reconstruction residual may under-emphasize, including sharp rock edges and mottled surfaces.
Fixed-coefficient multi-cue fusion uses a relative-depth \emph{edge} cue (image-relative depth
discontinuity), not a protrusion map.
Protrusion remains a candidate geomorph descriptor outside the fused score.
The depth-edge cue highlights regions that stand out from the surrounding near/far layout
rather than from appearance alone.
Cue maps therefore highlight unusual appearance or structure relative to a reference
distribution of ``normal'' terrain context.
No single cue is treated as a mineral detector, lithology classifier, or complete science
label; each map is an input to fusion and localization.
Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative}(b) shows the post-suppression combined anomaly map on a non-test
example.

\subsection{Monocular relative depth}
A dense monocular estimator provides an image-relative depth field for near/far
organization~\citep{ranftl2021dpt}.
Depth values are used as relative ordering cues, not as metric metres, absolute physical size,
or calibrated stereo geometry.
The same image can be ordered from nearer to farther surfaces, but those values are not
transferable across images as a common metric scale.
\Needspace{3\baselineskip}
Accordingly, no metric-depth claim is made in the present study.
Depth supports apparent size--distance policy and spatial structuring of candidates, and it
feeds the depth-edge cue used in fixed-coefficient multi-cue fusion.

\subsection{Entropy-weighted dynamic fusion}
Cue reliability can vary across illumination and terrain.
The ARTPS specification defines an entropy-weighted fusion rule: for normalized cue maps
\(A_i'\), Shannon entropy \(H_i=-\sum_k p_{ik}\log(p_{ik}+\varepsilon)\) yields
\(\tilde w_i=1/(H_i+\varepsilon)\) and \(w_i=\tilde w_i/\sum_j\tilde w_j\), so
\(A_{\mathrm{comb}}=\sum_i w_i A_i'\).
Low-entropy (peaked) maps receive higher weight.
Those normalized entropy weights sum to one.
The human-reviewed validation applies a
\emph{fixed-coefficient} linear mix instead of this entropy rule.
Let \(R\), \(E_D\), \(T\), \(L_D\), and \(F_D\) denote inference-normalized reconstruction
residual, relative-depth Sobel edge, texture / local-contrast, depth-Laplacian, and
fine-detail maps (percentile~$2$--$98$ per cue), and let \(P\) be the normalized proximity
map (near high).
The fixed-coefficient mix is
\[
A_{\mathrm{raw}}
=
0.50\,R + 0.30\,E_D + 0.20\,T + 0.08\,L_D + 0.12\,F_D,
\]
\[
P_{\mathrm{mix}}=0.55\,P+0.45\,(1-P),
\]
\[
A_{\mathrm{pre}}
=
\mathrm{clip}\!\bigl(A_{\mathrm{raw}}\,[0.5+0.5\,P_{\mathrm{mix}}],\,0,1\bigr).
\]
The values \(0.50/0.30/0.20/0.08/0.12\) are \emph{fixed fusion coefficients}: they are
implementation coefficients, they are not required to sum to one, and the output is
subsequently modulated and clipped.
They are distinct from the entropy-weighted architectural specification.
These are the fusion coefficients used to generate the human-reviewed image-level scores.
They are not claimed to be optimal or learned.
The proximity multiplier lies in a narrow band near \(0.75\), so far-field responses are
down-weighted rather than zeroed.
That fixed-coefficient mix is the Layer~B path used in the human-reviewed validation
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}).
Layer~C ranking---curiosity, soft similarity / diversity, and the Priority Buffer---is an
operational selection stack for onboard downlink; it is not applied when forming the
image-level score.
Conflating Layer~B image scores with Layer~C ranked target lists would misstate what the
human-reviewed validation measured.

\subsection{Candidate localization}
Fused anomaly responses are converted into discrete candidate regions via localization
(thresholding / connected components / peak extraction; implementation detail is in the
accompanying software).
Each candidate carries a score, spatial extent, and optional feature descriptor for diversity.
Candidates that fail validity checks after masks do not contribute to
the image-level score.
The image-level aggregation is the maximum among remaining valid candidates, not a mean over the
full anomaly map and not a curiosity-weighted sum.
Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative}(d) overlays only those remaining valid regions; it is not a
downlink ranking and does not apply curiosity, diversity, or the Priority Buffer.

\subsection{Operational false-positive suppression}
Rover imagery frequently contains distractors: rover body / hardware, strong boundary shadows,
frame edges, and telemetry overlays.
These regions can produce high reconstruction or texture responses that do not correspond to
science-interest targets.
ARTPS applies operational masks and suppressions so that high responses in these regions are
down-weighted or invalidated before ranking.

Boundary-connected shadow suppression targets dark, low-structure regions connected to the
image boundary, including cast-shadow wedges and similar artefacts, rather than suppressing
all dark pixels.
Rover-body and nearby hardware are excluded as self-view clutter.
Frame-edge and overlay / telemetry regions are invalidated where those signals are available.
A separate object-in-shadow check distinguishes a flat shadow artefact from a structured
candidate that happens to sit inside a shadowed area: suppressing the artefact must not
automatically discard the in-shadow object.
These masks act before image-level score aggregation.
They reduce invalid candidates; they do not establish that a surviving region is scientifically
relevant.

Only candidates that remain valid after these checks contribute to the image-level
score; Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative}(d) shows that candidate-support overlay on one non-test
example, not a curiosity-ranked downlink list.
A candidate that survives suppression is still only a localization hypothesis: it is not a
confirmed science target and is not automatically queued for downlink.
Suppression is policy-driven and inspectable; it is not a claim of perfect distractor rejection.

\subsection{Candidate and image-level score}
After localization and validity gates, each remaining candidate \(r\) receives a score
from pooled map responses and a classifier-derived known-value scalar.
Let \(F_r\) be the pooled combined-map response, \(D_r\) the pooled relative-depth value,
\(Q_r\) the proposal confidence, and \(K\) the image-global known-value scalar
(\(\arg\max\) class index divided by four when the classifier is enabled; not a per-box
classifier and not a claimed lithology label).
The ARTPS scoring function supports optional PaDiM and PatchCore responses.
These terms were disabled in the human-reviewed validation and therefore contribute zero to
the human-reviewed image-level score.
PaDiM/PatchCore is evaluated separately as a baseline.
The candidate equations are
\[
V_r=\mathrm{clip}(0.55\,K+0.25\,D_r+0.20\,F_r,0,1),
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_r=\mathrm{clip}\bigl(&0.50\,F_r+0.20\,P^{\mathrm{PaDiM}}_r+0.15\,P^{\mathrm{PC}}_r\\
&+0.10\,Q_r+0.05\,(1-D_r),0,1\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
S_r=\mathrm{clip}\bigl(A_r\,(0.70+0.30\,V_r),0,1\bigr).
\]
The image score is
\[
S_{\mathrm{image}}=\max_{r\in R_{\mathrm{valid}}} S_r,
\]
or \(S_{\mathrm{image}}=0\) when no valid candidate remains.
A slim recall-support pooling step may raise \(F_r\) for some proposal sources before these
equations; no additional score formula is introduced.

\subsection{Apparent size / image-relative size--distance policy}
Given relative depth and image extent, ARTPS applies an image-relative size--distance policy
to prefer candidates whose apparent size is consistent with the intended operational envelope
(e.g., avoiding both tiny noise peaks and unrealistically large blobs under relative depth
ordering).
This is a relative heuristic, not a physical size measurement in metres.

\subsection{Operational ranking sequence}
Candidates that remain valid after perception and masks enter an operational ranking
stack for onboard downlink.
The sequence is: a surviving candidate receives a curiosity score; a soft similarity penalty
then reduces its priority among already selected items; the Priority Buffer retains a
high-anomaly candidate that lost that diversity contest for later reconsideration.
This operational ranking path is not part of the image-level score.

\subsection{Curiosity score}
For operational ranking, ARTPS computes a curiosity score as a non-negative weighted
combination of available region cues (known-value / classifier term, reconstruction or
anomaly response, optional combined-map, depth variance, and roughness),
\[
C(r)=\sum_i w_i S_i(r),\qquad w_i\ge 0.
\]
Default deployed weights emphasize known-value and anomaly terms.
\(C(r)\) is \emph{not} the image-level score.

\subsection{Feature-space diversity}
To avoid redundant near-duplicates in the priority list, ARTPS applies a soft similarity
penalty in feature space to the fused candidate score \(S_r\).
If \(s(r)\) is the maximum cosine similarity to already selected items and
\(\lambda\in[0,1]\),
\[
S'_r=S_r\,(1-\lambda s(r)).
\]
Diversity improves coverage of distinct visual modes under limited downlink slots.
This ranking step is not applied when computing the image-level score.

\subsection{Priority Buffer}
Aggressive diversity down-weighting can deprioritize rare but potentially valuable candidates.
The Priority Buffer retains high-anomaly candidates that lose the diversity contest:
\[
r\in\mathcal{B}\iff S_r>\tau_{\mathrm{high}}\ \mathrm{and}\ \bigl(S_r-S'_r\bigr)>\tau_\Delta.
\]
Only candidates that remain valid after operational masks enter this buffer; the buffer does
not recover mask-invalidated regions.
The buffer is a recovery mechanism for second-chance re-evaluation, not a guarantee that
every rare target is recovered, and it is not part of the image-level score.

\subsection{Runtime and safety-aware fail-closed behaviour}
ARTPS is intended for edge-oriented operation with bounded compute.
Runtime processing returns an explicit no-selection outcome when evidence is insufficient.
Evaluation is aborted when required inputs or predefined evaluation conditions are incomplete,
rather than emitting an unverified priority list.
Safety bounds are stated in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
