\section{ARTPS Methodology}
This section describes the ARTPS pipeline as an architectural specification.
Quantitative performance claims appear only in Section~\ref{sec:results};
method prose here does not invent metrics.

\subsection{System overview}
ARTPS ingests a single RGB field image and produces a ranked list of candidate targets, or an
explicit no-selection outcome when evidence is insufficient.
Primary modules are: (i)~multi-cue anomaly responses, (ii)~monocular relative depth,
(iii)~multi-cue fusion (entropy-weighted specification; frozen evaluation uses fixed weights),
(iv)~candidate localization, (v)~operational false-positive
suppression, (vi)~apparent size / image-relative size--distance policy,
(vii)~feature-space diversity, (viii)~Priority Buffer, and (ix)~runtime fail-safe behaviour
with evaluation-integrity checks.
The frozen image-level score, implemented as
\texttt{max\_valid\_candidate\_after\_masks}, is the maximum score among candidates that remain
valid after operational masks and suppressions.
This score definition is held fixed for \texttt{independent\_eval\_v1} and frozen
\texttt{independent\_eval\_v1\_1} scoring and must not be silently redefined between
protocol versions.
Curiosity ranking, feature-space diversity, and the Priority Buffer are operational
selection mechanisms; they are \emph{not} applied on the frozen image-level score.
Optional Mars-oriented enhancement may precede cue extraction; it is preprocessing, not a
detection metric, and Real-ESRGAN is off in the frozen evaluation profile used here.

\subsection{Multi-cue visual anomaly detection}
ARTPS forms visual anomaly responses from embedding- or reconstruction-oriented cues aligned
with industrial anomaly practice~\citep{defard2020padim,roth2022patchcore}.
A reconstruction residual emphasizes appearance that the autoencoder cannot explain from
typical terrain context.
Complementary texture and local-contrast cues capture high-frequency structure that a
reconstruction residual may under-emphasize, including sharp rock edges and mottled surfaces.
A depth-edge / protrusion cue, derived from the monocular relative-depth field, highlights
regions that stand out from the surrounding near/far layout rather than from appearance alone.
Cue maps therefore highlight unusual appearance or structure relative to a reference
distribution of ``normal'' terrain context.
No single cue is treated as a mineral detector, lithology classifier, or complete science
label; each map is an input to fusion and localization.
Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative}(b) shows one anomaly-related cue on a non-test example.

\subsection{Monocular relative depth}
A dense monocular estimator provides an image-relative depth field for near/far
organization~\citep{ranftl2021dpt}.
Depth values are used as relative ordering cues, not as metric metres, absolute physical size,
or calibrated stereo geometry.
The same image can be ordered from nearer to farther surfaces, but those values are not
transferable across images as a common metric scale.
Metric depth claims are forbidden in this manuscript and in evaluation contracts.
Depth supports apparent size--distance policy and spatial structuring of candidates, and it
feeds the depth-edge cue used in frozen multi-cue fusion.
Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative}(c) shows only this image-relative near/far field.

\subsection{Entropy-weighted dynamic fusion}
Cue reliability can vary across illumination and terrain.
The ARTPS specification defines an entropy-weighted fusion rule: for normalized cue maps
\(A_i'\), Shannon entropy \(H_i=-\sum_k p_{ik}\log(p_{ik}+\varepsilon)\) yields
\(\tilde w_i=1/(H_i+\varepsilon)\) and \(w_i=\tilde w_i/\sum_j\tilde w_j\), so
\(A_{\mathrm{comb}}=\sum_i w_i A_i'\).
Low-entropy (peaked) maps receive higher weight.
% TODO(results): report fusion ablations only after independent\_eval\_v1 metrics exist.
The current detection core used for frozen independent-eval scoring applies a
\emph{fixed-weight} linear mix of reconstruction, depth-edge, and texture cues instead of
this entropy rule.
That fixed-weight mix is the measured Layer~B path in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}.
Layer~C ranking---curiosity, soft similarity / diversity, and the Priority Buffer---is an
operational selection stack for onboard downlink; it is not applied when forming the frozen
image-level score.
Conflating Layer~B image scores with Layer~C ranked target lists would misstate what the
supplementary validation actually measured.
Static equal-weight baselines remain available for protocol comparison but are not asserted
here as superior without evidence.

\subsection{Candidate localization}
Fused anomaly responses are converted into discrete candidate regions via localization
(thresholding / connected components / peak extraction---implementation detail retained in
code).
Each candidate carries a score, spatial extent, and optional feature descriptor for diversity.
Candidates that fail validity checks after masks do not contribute to
the frozen image-level score.
The frozen aggregation is the maximum among remaining valid candidates, not a mean over the
full anomaly map and not a curiosity-weighted sum.
Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative}(d) overlays only those remaining valid regions; it is not a
downlink ranking and does not apply curiosity, diversity, or the Priority Buffer.

\subsection{Operational false-positive suppression}
Rover imagery frequently contains distractors: rover body / hardware, strong boundary shadows,
frame edges, and telemetry overlays.
These regions can produce high reconstruction or texture responses that do not correspond to
science-interest targets.
ARTPS applies operational masks and suppressions so that high responses in these regions are
down-weighted or invalidated before ranking.
Typical checks include rover-hardware / body masks, strong boundary-shadow suppression, frame
edge invalidation, and overlay / telemetry rejection where those signals are available.
Only candidates that remain valid after these checks contribute to the frozen image-level
score; Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative}(d) shows that valid-candidate overlay on one non-test
example, not a curiosity-ranked downlink list.
A candidate that survives suppression is still only a localization hypothesis: it is not a
confirmed science target and is not automatically queued for downlink.
Suppression is policy-driven and auditable; it is not a claim of perfect distractor rejection.

\subsection{Apparent size / image-relative size--distance policy}
Given relative depth and image extent, ARTPS applies an image-relative size--distance policy
to prefer candidates whose apparent size is consistent with the intended operational envelope
(e.g., avoiding both tiny noise peaks and unrealistically large blobs under relative depth
ordering).
This is a relative heuristic, not a physical size measurement in metres.

\subsection{Curiosity score}
For operational ranking, ARTPS computes a curiosity score as a non-negative weighted
combination of available region cues (known-value / classifier term, reconstruction or
anomaly response, optional combined-map, depth variance, and roughness),
\[
C(r)=\sum_i w_i S_i(r),\qquad w_i\ge 0.
\]
Default deployed weights emphasize known-value and anomaly terms.
\(C(r)\) is \emph{not} the frozen image-level score.

\subsection{Feature-space diversity}
To avoid redundant near-duplicates in the priority list, ARTPS applies a soft similarity
penalty in feature space.
If \(s(r)\) is the maximum cosine similarity to already selected items and
\(\lambda\in[0,1]\),
\[
C'(r)=C(r)\,(1-\lambda s(r)).
\]
Diversity improves coverage of distinct visual modes under limited downlink slots.

This ranking step is not applied when computing the frozen image-level score.

\subsection{Priority Buffer}
Aggressive diversity or suppression can discard rare but scientifically interesting peaks.
The Priority Buffer retains high-anomaly candidates that lose the diversity contest:
\[
r\in\mathcal{B}\iff C(r)>\tau_{\mathrm{high}}\ \mathrm{and}\ \bigl(C(r)-C'(r)\bigr)>\tau_\Delta.
\]
The buffer is a recovery mechanism for second-chance re-evaluation, not a guarantee that
every rare target is recovered, and it is not part of the frozen image-level score.

\subsection{Runtime and safety-aware fail-closed behaviour}
ARTPS is intended for edge-oriented operation with bounded compute.
Runtime processing may return an explicit no-selection outcome when evidence is insufficient.
Evaluation is aborted when required inputs or frozen protocol conditions are incomplete,
rather than emitting an unverified priority list.
Safety bounds are stated in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
