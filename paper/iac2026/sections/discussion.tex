\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
ARTPS is an operational target-prioritization architecture in which anomaly cues and relative
depth feed fusion, localization, suppression, diversity, and buffering, with explicit
no-selection when evidence is insufficient.
The design goal is structured prioritization under rover constraints, not unconstrained
claim of superior detection in all regimes.
Anomaly-only policies miss that a high cue is not automatically a science-interest target
and that distractors (shadows, hardware, borders) inflate responses.
Relative depth supplies near/far structure for apparent-size policy without metric
distance~\citep{ranftl2021dpt}.
Diversity management reduces redundant near-duplicates under limited downlink slots;
the Priority Buffer keeps high-anomaly candidates that the similarity penalty would otherwise
drop irreversibly.
These ranking mechanisms are specified for onboard selection; they are not part of the
image-level validation scores.
Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative} therefore shows Layer~B products (post-suppression combined map,
relative depth, valid candidates) rather than a Priority-Buffer or diversity-ranked target list.
Fig.~\ref{fig:cue-decomp} decomposes that same Layer~B path into the three primary
cues and the pre-suppression fused map; it does not introduce a new metric.
The example is score-blind in selection and illustrative in purpose: a high local cue or a
near-field blob does not by itself become a science target, and a single non-test frame
cannot stand in for a detection benchmark.
Read left to right, the panels separate appearance, the post-suppression combined map,
image-relative depth, and the candidates that remain after operational masks.
Corner brackets and, where available, a translucent support footprint on the overlay mark a
valid localization hypothesis under the image-level scoring path, not a pixel-level segmentation,
not a measured curiosity rank, and not a claim that the region is scientifically confirmed.

The entropy-weighted fusion rule remains the specified adaptive fusion mode.
The human-reviewed validation uses the fixed-coefficient image-level scoring configuration.
Table~\ref{tab:eval-tracks} summarizes the primary performance evaluation and the
human-reviewed validation.
The initial binary labels were produced by a classical-image heuristic with forced $180/180$
class balance.
A model-blind repeat-author review produced $340$ positive / $20$ negative labels.
The reviewed labels more directly reflect the adopted human annotation definition than the
initial heuristic label layer.
This observed prevalence is specific to the sampled Curiosity Mastcam domain and the
adopted science-interest annotation definition; it should not be generalized to
planetary imagery as a whole.
The human-reviewed corpus contains $340$ positive and $20$ negative images.
On the validation partition, the ARTPS image-level score achieved AUROC~$0.772$.
Because the validation partition contains only eight negative images and the predeclared
operating point yielded all-positive predictions, the result is interpreted primarily as
ranking evidence rather than as a calibrated binary-classification operating point.
The small number of negative validation examples also limits the precision of the reported
AUROC estimate.
Threshold-based F1 is not informative here because the predeclared operating point is
all-positive.
A future balanced challenge set or harder negative set may be useful for operating-point
evaluation.

The reported $28.1$\,FPS value is a lightweight-core workstation measurement and is not a
full neural-pipeline edge-hardware or flight-qualification result.
