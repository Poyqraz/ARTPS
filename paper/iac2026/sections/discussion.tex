\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
ARTPS is best read as an operational architecture: anomaly cues and relative depth feed
fusion, localization, suppression, diversity, and buffering, with explicit no-selection
when evidence is insufficient.
The design goal is structured prioritization under rover constraints, not unconstrained
claim of superior detection in all regimes.
Anomaly-only policies miss that a high cue is not automatically a science-interest target
and that distractors (shadows, hardware, borders) inflate responses.
Relative depth supplies near/far structure for apparent-size policy without metric
distance~\citep{ranftl2021dpt}.
Diversity management reduces redundant near-duplicates under limited downlink slots;
the Priority Buffer keeps high-anomaly candidates that the similarity penalty would otherwise
drop irreversibly.
These ranking mechanisms are specified for onboard selection; they are not part of the
frozen supplementary validation scores.
Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} therefore shows Layer~B products (cue, relative depth, valid
candidates) rather than a Priority-Buffer or diversity-ranked target list.
The example is score-blind in selection and illustrative in purpose: a high local cue or a
near-field blob does not by itself become a science target, and a single non-test frame
cannot stand in for a detection benchmark.
Read left to right, the panels separate appearance, an anomaly-related response, image-relative
depth, and the candidates that remain after operational masks.
A box on the overlay is a valid localization hypothesis under the frozen image-score path,
not a measured curiosity rank and not a claim that the region is scientifically confirmed.

The entropy-weighted fusion rule remains the architectural specification; the current frozen
evaluation uses fixed-weight multi-cue fusion.
Table~\ref{tab:eval-tracks} summarizes the historical versus supplementary evaluation tracks.
The initial \texttt{independent\_eval\_v1} binary labels were produced by a classical-image
heuristic with forced $180/180$ class balance.
A model-blind repeat-author review produced $340$ positive / $20$ negative labels.
\texttt{independent\_eval\_v1\_1} more directly reflects the adopted human annotation
definition than the initial heuristic label layer.
This observed prevalence is specific to the sampled Curiosity Mastcam domain and the
adopted science-interest annotation definition; it should not be generalized to
planetary imagery as a whole.
Frozen-score AUROC~$0.772$ indicates ranking separation under that definition, not an
operating-point classification result.
Threshold-based F1 is not informative here because the predeclared operating point is
all-positive.
A future balanced challenge set or harder negative set may be useful for operating-point
evaluation.
This label-audit finding is confined to the supplementary validation benchmark; it
does not imply that the historical accepted-abstract ARTPS experiment suffered the same
issue.
Proxy analyses remain secondary and must not migrate into abstract headlines.

Hardware timing (including planned Jetson profiles) is engineering measurement, not
certification evidence.
